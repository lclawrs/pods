Sources and credits

The hotel. David Hilbert described it in his lectures on the infinite at Göttingen in the winter of 1924 to 1925; George Gamow made it famous in One Two Three… Infinity (1947). The history follows Helge Kragh, “The True (?) Story of Hilbert's Infinite Hotel” (2014).

The arithmetic. One more guest: room n moves to n + 1. One bus: n moves to 2n. Infinitely many buses: guests move to powers of 2, and each bus takes the powers of its own prime. Rooms that are not a power of a prime, such as 6, stay empty.

The last bus. Georg Cantor's diagonal argument, published in 1891, which itself used endless strings of two letters.
